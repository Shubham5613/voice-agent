# What gets a "yes" on a card sales call: evidence and how the agent uses it

Compiled 2026-10-26. Companion to `research_indian_card_telesales.md` (annoyance + regulation) and
`research_ai_disclosure_openers.md` (AI disclosure, opener A/B data). Each item: the evidence, how strong it is,
and what the agent does about it. Vendor data is marked as such.

## 1. The first ten seconds: reason, context, no "bad time"
| Evidence | Strength | Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Gong, 300M cold calls: "Did I catch you at a bad time?" 2.15% success; "How's your day going?" 7.6%; permission opener with context 11.18%; "heard the name tossed around?" 11.24%. [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls/) | vendor, large N, B2B | Opener leads with identity + a specific reason + a thirty-second ask. Never "bad time?". |
| Gong, 90k calls: stating "the reason for my call" ≈2.1x success. [Gong cold-call guide](https://pages.gong.io/wp-content/uploads/Cold-Call-Guide.pdf) | vendor | The hook is always a reason ("you'd tapped the Swiggy offer", "pre-approved on your account"). |
| Langer, Blank & Chanowitz 1978: a stated reason ("because…") lifted compliance 60%→93% for small requests; placebic reasons stop working for big asks. [summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/brain-wise/201310/the-power-of-the-word-because-to-get-people-to-do-stuff) | peer-reviewed, small N | Small asks (thirty seconds, a WhatsApp line) always carry a reason; the big ask (take the card) carries real numbers, not a placebic reason. |
| Nooks, top-1% reps: 53% skip the permission opener and lead with a relevant statement; 71% personalise around the person, not the company; 83% pitch the outcome, not features. [Nooks](https://www.nooks.ai/blog-posts/how-the-top-1-of-reps-run-outbound-calls) | vendor, small sample | The value line is an outcome in rupees and lounge visits, personal to them. |
| Truecaller 2025: users ignore 81% of 140-series telemarketing calls. [via LinkedIn / Communications Today](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/?p=276197) | company claim | The first sentence must sound like the bank, be verifiable ("see it in the HDFC app"), and never like a campaign. |

## 2. Personalisation: helps when accurate and transparent, hurts when creepy
| Evidence | Strength | Agent |
|---|---|---|
| BCG: life-event based offers raised a retail bank's offer conversion 30%. [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/capabilities/marketing-sales/personalization) | consultant self-report | Relationship-aware opener (pre-approved vs pre-qualified), spend-category value line. |
| Clutch survey: 62% find ads that follow them creepy; 98% say transparency is key to trust; 43% share data for tangible benefit. [Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/ineffective-invasive-personalization-turn-customers-away/830339/) | US consumer survey | Categories and outcomes only, never merchants/amounts/dates; honest one-line answer to "are you tracking me?" with the opt-out. |
| Site Impact 2026: personalised marketing most often described as "repetitive" (45%). [Demand Gen Report](https://www.demandgenreport.com/?p=54352) | US survey | Identity once; value line once in the opener and again only when asked. |
| SaveSage 2025, 5,000 Indian cardholders: 60% unaware of perks like lounge access; 70% fail to maximise rewards. [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/savesages-pan-india-survey-reveals-70-of-credit-card-users-fail-to-maximise-rewards/) | Indian, decent N | Name one concrete perk they would otherwise miss, in numbers (twelve lounge visits), rather than "great rewards". |
| ANQ survey: ~60% of Indian users prioritise rewards when choosing a card. [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/nearly-60-of-users-prioritise-rewards-when-choosing-credit-survey) | vendor | Lead with the reward in their category. |
| Collinson: 44% of Indian millennials rank lounge access their top travel card benefit. [India Outbound](https://indiaoutbound.info/?p=105642) | vendor | Lounge visits are the headline for travel leads. |

## 3. Objections: acknowledge, one fact, one small step
| Evidence | Strength | Agent |
|---|---|---|
| GroMo (Indian card DSA platform) nine objections: "already have a card" → ask which, position a second card for the gap, never criticise; "annual fee" → show the waiver math against their spend; "I'll think about it" → ask what they'd want to be sure about (fee, approval, safety), then set a specific follow-up; "UPI is enough" → rewards, interest-free float, credit history, RuPay credit on UPI; "family says no" → offer to explain with the family present; "is this safe?" → show the process, never "trust me"; close with one small step, never "so will you buy?". [GroMo](https://gromo.in/blog/handle-credit-card-sales-objections/) | practitioner, Indian | All nine now in the playbook (the last four added 2026-10-26). |
| Callyzer: the best outcome of a hard objection is often a scheduled follow-up, not a forced close. [Callyzer](https://callyzer.co/blog/how-to-convince-customers-to-buy-a-credit-card/) | practitioner | Busy/later → summary on WhatsApp + a booked slot, never an exit question. |
| Call Centre Helper: acknowledge before answering or you sound like you are not listening. [CCH](https://www.callcentrehelper.com/handling-sales-objections-over-the-telephone-12219.htm) | practitioner | Back-channel then answer. |

## 4. Getting the yes: small steps, freedom, honest fee math
| Evidence | Strength | Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Foot-in-the-door beat door-in-the-face and hard-to-get in a phone field experiment (128 candidates). [Hogrefe](https://econtent.hogrefe.com/doi/10.1026/0932-4089/a000310) | peer-reviewed, small | Ladder: thirty seconds → WhatsApp summary → callback → yes after fee → last four. Each step is tiny. |
| "But you are free" (BYAF): Carpenter 2013 meta-analysis positive; Fillon et al. 2020 (52 experiments, N≈19.5k) g=0.44 but near zero in the seven low-risk studies. [Meta-Psychology](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2640) | contested | One honest "entirely your call" at the disclosure turn. Costs nothing, may help, matches the no-pressure stance. |
| Door-in-the-face produces verbal but not behavioural compliance (Feeley meta-analysis). [U Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2012/10/13763.html) | peer-reviewed | Never open with a premium card to "drop" to a cheaper one. Pitch the fitting card first. |
| Luo et al. 2019 (in the disclosure note): purchase rates fall 80% when AI is disclosed up front; the founder's instruction is disclose only if asked. | peer-reviewed field RCT | Disclose if asked, plainly, then offer a human. |
| Outlook Money, Jupiter: "lifetime free" is conditional or a trap; the honest framing is the waiver threshold. [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/lifetime-free-credit-cards-whats-the-hidden-cost) · [Jupiter](https://jupiter.money/blog/?p=3570) | journalism | Never "lifetime free". Fee outlook says whether THEY cross the waiver and that the welcome gift offsets the joining fee, or says plainly that they may not. |

## 5. What is NOT supported (so we do not do it)
- No published evidence that "no-oriented questions" or labelling raise callback rates on sales calls; the sources are training blogs. [example](https://www.11x.ai/guides/chris-voss-sales-training-method)
- No verified Indian card telesales conversion benchmark exists in public. GroMo's "5% to 25%" is a vendor claim.
- Fake urgency: not used. Only a real offer validity date from the lead record, said once.

## 7. The honest answer to "how do we make them say yes"
Nothing in the evidence suggests a phrase that flips a no. What moves the number is: a reason in the first sentence, a personal outcome in numbers, one acknowledged objection answered with a fact, a next step so small it is easier to accept than refuse, and no exits offered. The agent now does each of these. The remaining lever is measurement: run the arena, count where people drop by stage, and change one thing at a time (Apollo's guidance: 50–100 connects per variant before declaring a winner, [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/what-are-techniques-for-ab-testing-call-scripts-or-voicemail-messages)).
